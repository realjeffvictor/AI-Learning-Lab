## The scenario

A team of three developers is building a **note-taking web app** for a school. About 200 students will use it. Students sign in, write notes, search their notes and share a note with a classmate. The team has six weeks and one part-time person who looks after servers.

The team is split. One developer wants a **single application with one database**. Another wants **several small services** (accounts, notes, search, sharing) so they can scale independently.

## What to write

Write an **architecture decision record** in the documentation box. Use these headings:

1. **Context.** What is being built, for whom, and what limits the team?
2. **Options.** At least two architectures, described clearly enough that someone else could draw them.
3. **Trade-offs.** Compare the options on cost, complexity, risk and fit for *this* team. Name what each option makes easier and what it makes harder.
4. **Decision.** Which option, and why. Be specific to the scenario.
5. **Consequences and risks.** What follows from your choice, including the downsides you are accepting.
6. **Revisit.** How you would check the decision is working, and what would make you change it.

## Using AI well

You may use an AI assistant to explore options and challenge your thinking. The decision and the reasoning must be yours:

- **Direct:** give the assistant the whole scenario and the constraints, and ask for alternatives and objections.
- **Verify:** check any claim you are unsure about, such as costs and performance.
- **Own:** in the reflection box, say which parts of the final record are your judgment and where the AI changed your mind.

## How this is assessed

This is a reviewed task. A reviewer reads your record and scores your understanding, the quality of your reasoning, how you would verify the decision, and whether you can defend it as your own.
